Khord is an end-to-end encrypted messenger built on a split-trust
architecture: no single server operator can read messages, identify
users, or observe who talks to whom.

Two independent servers each hold half the picture. The Key Server
stores identity keys and pre-key bundles but never sees message
traffic. The Relay Server routes opaque encrypted blobs between
per-contact mailboxes but never learns user identities. Neither can
reconstruct the whole on its own, and the two can be operated by
different organisations in different jurisdictions.

Both servers are open source and self-hostable. The app ships
pointing at the community servers (keys.khord.org, relay.khord.org)
but you can switch to your own from the in-app settings.

Encryption
- End-to-end encryption using the Signal Protocol (X3DH key
  agreement + Double Ratchet), implemented on libsodium primitives.
- Forward secrecy and post-compromise recovery.

Identity
- Your identity is a cryptographic key, derived from a BIP39 seed
  phrase. No phone number, no email, no account.
- The same seed phrase restores your identity on a new device.
- Contact fingerprint verification with key-change warnings.

Features
- One-to-one and group messaging (groups are entirely client-side;
  servers have no concept of groups).
- Contacts added by QR code or contact link — no server-side
  contact discovery.
- Local full-text message search.
- Message editing, quote-replies, contact blocking and muting.
- Panic button: instant, total wipe of all data on the device.
- Encrypted local storage (SQLCipher + Android Keystore).

Status
Khord is a proof of concept under active development. The
cryptographic implementation has not yet been independently
security-audited. Do not rely on it for high-risk communication
until an audit has been completed.
